## Reflection

The backlog shows the product being built in a deliberate order: understand the problem, validate the business model, design the experience, then build authentication, the profile, the CV editor, persistence, AI tools and subscriptions. Each item depends on the one before it, so the ordering mattered more than I expected — the design work in Sprint 5 was only useful because the problem statement in Sprint 1 and the backlog refinement in Sprint 4 had already narrowed what we were designing.

The earliest retrospective said the initial concept was too broad, and the backlog is where we acted on that. We narrowed the first release to a reusable career profile and a guided CV workflow and recorded everything else as deferred in the charter. That discipline kept later sprints focused, although the open items at the end of Sprint 14 show how much remained — mapping selected projects into the saved CV and defining the Free, Starter and Pro entitlements were both still in progress when the sprint closed.

**What I would change:** I would add estimates and explicit priority labels such as MoSCoW to each item so the order is justified, not just recorded. Reading the due-date table now, several cards sit together on the same date — three separate research cards all due 16 July, five documentation cards all due 18–19 June — and without estimates or priorities it is hard to tell whether they were genuinely parallel work or simply cards that were created together and dated to the same deadline. Adding an estimate and a priority to each card at refinement would have made the backlog easier to defend and easier to reorder when capacity changed.
